# DA-first Analytics Methodology

## Scope and evidence boundary

This sprint analyzes the existing Sprint 4A pilot only: 50 private JD profiles, 125 synthetic candidate profiles, and 1,000 sampled unique candidate–JD pairs. Pair labels and match features come from Pair Label Policy v2. They are useful for auditing and comparing rule behavior, but they are not real recruiting outcomes or independent ground truth.

The SQLite database and Power BI exports remain private because the JD source license has not been verified. No raw JD description or resume text is loaded into the analytics database; the database still contains derived job attributes and must not be published.

## KPI framework

| KPI | Numerator | Denominator | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive pair rate | Pairs with `positive` rule label | Sampled pairs in scope | Rule-policy mix, not hire or recruiter-approval rate |
| Viable pair rate | `positive` + `partial` pairs | Sampled pairs in scope | Broader policy-defined candidate pool |
| Required-skill coverage | Policy-v2 matched required-skill credit | Required skills on the JD | Rule-derived pair fit |
| JD positive coverage | JDs with at least one positive sampled candidate | All pilot JDs, including any zero-pair JD | Whether the sampled synthetic pool contains a positive rule match |
| Candidate positive coverage | Candidates with at least one positive sampled JD | All pilot candidates | Whether a synthetic candidate has a positive rule match |
| Skill missing-pair rate | Relevant pairs where required skill is recorded missing | Sampled pairs attached to JDs requiring the skill | Simulated skill bottleneck; minimum denominator should be shown |
| Experience-gap distribution | Pairs in each candidate YOE minus JD minimum YOE bucket | Pairs with known JD minimum YOE; unknown is reported separately | Experience alignment in the selected pilot |
| Field missingness | Null/unknown values for a named field | Entities in that table | Completeness, with selection effects documented |

Rates are computed only after selecting the reporting scope. Power BI should always display the denominator or sample size beside a rate.

## Offline strategy comparison — next sprint

The offline comparison should use the same fixed JD/candidate eligibility pool for every strategy and produce a ranked candidate list per JD.

Proposed strategies:

1. **YOE-heavy baseline:** higher weight on meeting minimum experience and seniority.
2. **Skills-first treatment candidate:** higher weight on required-skill coverage, with YOE as a guardrail rather than the dominant term.

Evaluation design:

- Freeze strategy definitions before reading comparative results.
- Use a group-disjoint evaluation set with independently reviewed relevance judgments when available. Rule labels can support pipeline debugging only.
- Primary offline metric: `NDCG@5` or `Precision@5` against independently reviewed relevance.
- Secondary metrics: JD coverage at K, candidate exposure/coverage, average required-skill coverage at K, severe YOE-gap rate at K, and segment results by JD family.
- Unit of analysis: JD. Compute one metric per JD, then compare paired strategy differences so repeated candidates/pairs are not treated as independent observations.
- Uncertainty: paired bootstrap confidence intervals resampling JDs; report the mean/median paired difference and interval. If the number of independently reviewed JDs is small, emphasize intervals and raw JD-level differences rather than p-values.
- Guardrails: no material increase in severe experience gaps, no collapse in candidate exposure, and no job-family segment with a practically large regression hidden by the overall mean.
- Multiple metrics: declare one primary metric and treat secondary tests as diagnostic; adjust or clearly label exploratory multiplicity.

The current Sprint 4A labels cannot support a claim that one strategy improves real matching quality because those labels are produced by the same rule features that the strategies would use.

## True online A/B test framework — design only

An A/B test becomes appropriate only after Smash has live eligible traffic, stable logging, a treatment strategy ready for production, and an outcome that is not generated by the matching rule itself.

### Randomization and exposure

- Randomize at a unit that avoids interference. Recruiter or hiring-team randomization is safer when the same recruiter repeatedly sees candidates; request/session randomization may be acceptable only if carryover is controlled.
- Persist assignment, log eligibility before assignment, and analyze by intention to treat.
- Run an A/A test or instrumentation validation before the treatment test.
- Prevent candidates or JDs from being exposed inconsistently when that would contaminate behavior.

### Metrics

- Primary outcome candidate: recruiter shortlist rate among eligible exposed recommendation sets, defined from an independently logged recruiter action.
- Secondary: qualified-contact rate, time to first shortlist, candidate coverage/exposure, required-skill coverage at K, and recruiter engagement.
- Guardrails: severe YOE-gap exposure, duplicate recommendation rate, latency/error rate, opt-out/complaint rate, and segment-level fairness diagnostics where legally and ethically appropriate.
- Longer-funnel outcomes such as interview, offer, and placement require sufficient follow-up and explicit attribution rules.

### Power and analysis

- Estimate baseline rate and cluster/variance structure from pre-experiment traffic; choose minimum detectable effect with business owners.
- Calculate sample size for the actual randomization unit, inflating for clustering and expected attrition. Do not derive power from the synthetic pilot.
- Pre-specify duration, stopping rules, exclusions, metric windows, and the primary estimand.
- Use confidence intervals and effect sizes. For clustered assignment, use cluster-robust inference or aggregate at the randomization unit.
- Check sample-ratio mismatch, logging loss, novelty/learning effects, and heterogeneous effects without turning exploratory segments into uncorrected success claims.

No randomized experiment has been run in this project, so there are no A/B test results or business-lift claims in the current deliverables.
